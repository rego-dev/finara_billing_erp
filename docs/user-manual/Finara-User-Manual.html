01

Getting Started

How to sign in, read the dashboard, and move between businesses before touching any accounting module.

1.1 · Login screen

Signing In

Authenticates you into Finara with a company email and password. Sessions are secured with JWT access/refresh tokens (bcrypt-hashed passwords), and every login attempt — successful, failed, or blocked — is written to the Audit Trail.

Email AddressThe address your administrator registered for you.
PasswordUse the eye icon to reveal/hide it while typing.
Forgot password?Sends a reset link (valid 1 hour) without ever confirming whether an email exists in the system.

How to do it

  1. Open Finara and enter your Email Address and Password.
  2. Click Sign In.
  3. On success you land on the Dashboard with a "Welcome back" toast; on failure you'll see a generic "Invalid credentials" message.
  4. If you mistype your password 5 times in a row, the account locks for 15 minutes — wait it out or use "Forgot password?"

Tips

  • Bookmark the login page — Finara redirects you straight back to it whenever your session (default 8 hours) expires.

Watch out for

  • 5 failed attempts locks the account for 15 minutes, even if the 6th attempt would have been correct.
1.2 · Sidebar → Dashboard

Reading the Dashboard

A read-only landing page that summarizes the health of the currently active business the moment you log in — no filters, nothing to configure.

Stat cardsOpen Receivables, Open Payables, This Month Revenue, and Net Income (MTD), each with a supporting count.
MTD chartA bar chart of Revenue vs. Expenses vs. Net Income for the current month.
Alerts panelSurfaces only what needs attention: Overdue Receivables, Overdue Payables, and Draft Journal Entries awaiting review. Shows "All clear!" when there's nothing to flag.
Quick countsActive Customers, Active Vendors, Active Employees.

How to do it

  1. Log in — the Dashboard is the default landing page.
  2. Scan the four stat cards for a same-day snapshot.
  3. Check the Alerts panel first — it is the fastest way to see what is overdue or waiting on you.
  4. Use the counts and alerts as jumping-off points into the relevant module (e.g. click through to Accounts Payable if bills are overdue).
1.3 · Header → Business switcher

Switching Businesses

Finara is multi-business: one login can have access to several companies (e.g. separate legal entities), each with its own books, Chart of Accounts, and documents. The switcher in the header changes which business's data every other screen shows.

Single-business usersSee a static label with the business name — there's nothing to switch.
Multi-business usersSee a dropdown listing every business you have access to, with a checkmark on the active one.

How to do it

  1. Click the business name in the header.
  2. Pick a different business from the dropdown.
  3. Every module — Dashboard, Accounts, Journal, Reports — immediately reflects the newly selected business's data.

Watch out for

  • You only see businesses an admin has explicitly granted you access to via Settings → Businesses — ADMIN users see all businesses automatically.
02

Accounting

The backbone of the system: the Chart of Accounts every other module posts to, the General Ledger where those postings live, and templates that automate repeating entries.

2.1 · Sidebar → Accounting → Chart of Accounts

Chart of Accounts

Maintains the PFRS-aligned master list of GL accounts (Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Revenue, Expense) that every invoice, bill, payslip, and journal entry ultimately posts against.

Account CodeOptional — leave blank and Finara auto-numbers it (steps of 10, e.g. 1010, 1020) inside the standard PH ranges: 1xxx Assets, 2xxx Liabilities, 3xxx Equity, 4xxx Revenue, 5xxx–6xxx Expenses. Locked once the account exists.
Account TypeASSET, LIABILITY, EQUITY, REVENUE, or EXPENSE — cannot be changed after creation.
Normal BalanceDEBIT or CREDIT — auto-set from the type, but overridable.
Parent / Header AccountOnly root (non-child) accounts can be parents, giving one level of grouping in the tree view.

How to do it

  1. Click New Account.
  2. Choose the Account Type (Normal Balance auto-fills).
  3. Enter an Account Name; optionally set a custom code, a parent account, and a description.
  4. Click Create Account.
  5. To retire an account, edit it and mark it inactive rather than deleting — deletion is blocked once it has any transactions.

Watch out for

  • An account with existing journal lines cannot be deleted — Finara returns "Cannot delete account with existing transactions."
  • Only ADMIN/MANAGER can create or edit accounts; only ADMIN can delete one.
2.2 · Sidebar → Accounting → General Ledger

General Ledger (Journal Entries)

Where every double-entry transaction in the business is recorded, reviewed as a DRAFT, posted to become part of the official ledger, or — if truly necessary — voided under a second layer of authorization.

Date / Reference / DescriptionHeader details for the entry; Reference is typically an OR or document number.
Journal linesMinimum two lines, each with an Account, a Debit or Credit amount, and an optional memo.
Balance indicatorLive "✓ Balanced" / "✗ Off by X.XX" readout — Save is disabled until debits equal credits.

How to do it

  1. Click New Entry and set the Date and Description.
  2. Add lines with + Add Line, picking an account and a debit or credit amount for each.
  3. Confirm the totals row reads "✓ Balanced."
  4. Click Save Entry — it is created as DRAFT.
  5. From the list, click the check-circle Post icon to commit it to POSTED — only posted entries reach the Trial Balance, Income Statement, and Balance Sheet.

Tips

  • Reports (Trial Balance, Income Statement, Balance Sheet) only ever include POSTED entries — a DRAFT entry is invisible to them until it's posted.

Watch out for

  • Voiding is permanent and requires a mandatory reason plus an ADMIN password on a second confirmation screen — a voided entry can never be re-posted.
  • Only DRAFT entries can be edited; POSTED or VOIDED entries are locked.
2.3 · Sidebar → Accounting → Recurring Entries

Recurring Entries

Journal-entry templates (e.g. monthly rent, amortization) that generate real, balanced journal entries on a schedule instead of you re-typing them every period.

FrequencyWEEKLY, MONTHLY, QUARTERLY, or ANNUALLY.
Start / End DateStart Date becomes the first "next run" date; End Date (optional) auto-deactivates the template once passed.
Template linesSame shape as a Journal Entry — must balance before the template can be saved.

How to do it

  1. Click New Template, name it, and choose a Frequency and Start Date.
  2. Build the balanced line template (accounts + debit/credit amounts).
  3. Click Create — the template goes live with "next run" set to the Start Date.
  4. Click Run All Due to fire every template whose run date has passed, or Run Now on one row to post it immediately.
  5. Each run posts a normal journal entry and advances the template's next-run date automatically.

Watch out for

  • A run dated earlier than the business's configured "books start date" is skipped rather than posted, with an explanatory message instead of a fabricated entry.
03

Sales — Accounts Receivable

Everything involved in billing customers: quoting, invoicing, collecting payment, and tracking who still owes you money.

3.1 · Sidebar → Sales → Accounts Receivable → Invoices

Invoices

Creates and tracks sales invoices billed to customers — the core of the AR ledger — and records collections against them as payments come in.

Customer / Invoice Date / Due DateDue Date defaults to Invoice Date + 30 days if left blank.
Line itemsRevenue Account, Description, Quantity, Unit Price, and a VAT code per line: VAT (12%), ZERO-rated, or EXEMPT.
Totals panelSubtotal, Output VAT (12%), and Total Invoice Amount recompute live as you edit lines.

How to do it

  1. Click New Invoice and select (or add) a customer.
  2. Set the Invoice Date; adjust the auto-filled Due Date if needed.
  3. Add line items, choosing a Revenue Account and VAT code per line.
  4. Review the computed totals, then click Create Invoice.
  5. When payment arrives, open the invoice and click Record Collection: enter the amount (or use the 25/50/75/100% shortcuts), payment method, and reference number.

Tips

  • GCash, Maya, PESONet, InstaPay, Bank Transfer, Check, and Cash are all supported collection methods, each mapped to the correct cash/bank GL account automatically.

Watch out for

  • Voiding is only allowed while paidAmount is exactly zero — once any collection is recorded, you must reverse the collection first.
3.2 · Sidebar → Sales → Accounts Receivable → Quotations

Quotations

Drafts price quotes for customers before committing to a GL-posting invoice — nothing on a quotation touches the ledger until it is converted.

Customer / Quotation Date / Valid UntilValid Until defaults to +30 days.
LinesItem (typing a stocked item name auto-fills its price), Description, Qty, Unit Price, VAT code — no GL account yet.

How to do it

  1. Click New Quotation, pick a customer, and add line items.
  2. Save as DRAFT, edit as needed, then click Mark Sent once delivered to the customer.
  3. Record the customer response with Accept or Reject.
  4. When ready to bill, click Convert to Invoice: assign a revenue account to every line (required — the button stays disabled otherwise), confirm the Due Date, and submit.
  5. A real, GL-posted AR Invoice is created and the quotation is marked CONVERTED and linked to it.

Watch out for

  • Only DRAFT quotations can be edited, and a CONVERTED quotation can never be deleted or re-converted.
3.3 · Sidebar → Sales → Accounts Receivable → Customers

Customers

Maintains the AR customer master list and gives a one-click view of each customer's billing and collection standing.

Customer CodeAuto-generated as CUS-001, CUS-002... if left blank; locked after creation.
Name (required), TIN, Address, Contact Person, Phone, EmailEverything else is optional.

How to do it

  1. Click New Customer and enter at least the name.
  2. Save — the customer is now selectable when creating Invoices or Quotations.
  3. Click any customer card to open a detail drawer with Total Billed, Outstanding balance, Collection Rate, and recent invoices.
  4. Toggle Active/Inactive from edit mode — inactive customers no longer appear in the invoice-creation picker.
3.4 · Sidebar → Sales → Accounts Receivable → AR Aging

AR Aging

A read-only report bucketing everything customers owe by how overdue it is — the fastest way to spot collection risk.

BucketsCurrent, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, Over 90 days.
Included documentsOnly OPEN/PARTIAL/OVERDUE invoices — fully paid and voided invoices are excluded.

How to do it

  1. Open AR Aging — the report builds automatically as of today.
  2. Review the KPI cards: Total Receivable, Not Yet Due, Total Overdue, and a color-coded Collection Risk percentage.
  3. Switch between Bar and Pie chart views of the bucket totals.
  4. Expand a customer row in the detail table to see exactly which invoices are driving their balance.
  5. Click Refresh to recompute against the current date.

Watch out for

  • Any balance in the Over 90 days bucket triggers a banner suggesting collection-agency engagement or bad-debt write-off.

Purchases — Accounts Payable

Recording what the business owes vendors, paying it, and the surrounding purchase-order, petty-cash, and cash-advance workflows.

4.1 · Sidebar → Purchases → Accounts Payable → Bills

Bills

Records vendor purchase invoices and tracks payments against them — the AP counterpart to Sales Invoices.

Vendor / Bill Date / Due DateDue Date defaults to Bill Date + 30 days.
Line itemsExpense/Asset Account, Description, VAT code (VAT/ZERO/EXEMPT), Qty, Unit Price.

How to do it

  1. Click New Bill, select (or add) a vendor, and set the dates.
  2. Add line items with the correct expense/asset account and VAT code.
  3. Review the computed totals, then save — the bill posts to the GL automatically (expense debited, Input VAT debited if applicable, Accounts Payable credited).
  4. When you pay it, open the bill and click Record Payment: enter amount, method, and reference.

Watch out for

  • Voiding a bill is only allowed while nothing has been paid against it yet — reverse payments first.
4.2 · Sidebar → Purchases → Accounts Payable → Vendors

Vendors

Maintains the AP vendor master list used across Bills and Purchase Orders, with a quick view of each vendor's billing history.

Vendor CodeAuto-generated as VEN-001, VEN-002... at creation; editable only then.
Name (required), TIN, Address, Contact Person, Phone, EmailEverything else is optional.

How to do it

  1. Click New Vendor and enter at least the name.
  2. Save — the vendor is now selectable on Bills and Purchase Orders.
  3. Click a vendor card to review Total Billed, Outstanding balance, and recent bills.

Watch out for

  • Inactive vendors disappear from the Bill/PO creation picker but their history stays intact — there is no vendor-delete option in Finara.
4.3 · Sidebar → Purchases → Accounts Payable → AP Aging

AP Aging

A read-only report bucketing everything owed to vendors by how overdue it is.

BucketsCurrent, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, Over 90 days.
Included documentsOnly OPEN/PARTIAL/OVERDUE bills — Void and Paid bills are excluded.

How to do it

  1. Open AP Aging — the report builds automatically as of today.
  2. Review the KPI cards and the bucket-summary bar chart.
  3. Expand a vendor row to see which specific bills are aging.
  4. Click Refresh to recompute against today.

Watch out for

  • Any balance over 90 days overdue triggers a banner recommending vendor negotiation or escalation.
4.4 · Sidebar → Purchases → Purchase Orders

Purchase Orders

Manages the full purchasing lifecycle — from a draft order, through receiving goods, to either a formal AP Bill or a direct petty-cash payment.

Vendor / Order Date / Expected DeliveryHeader details.
LinesDescription, Qty, Unit Price, and — required before billing — an Expense Account per line.
Scan & ImportUploads a photo of a paper PO; OCR pre-fills vendor, dates, and line items for review before import.

How to do it

  1. Click New PO (or use Scan & Import for a paper form) and fill in vendor and lines.
  2. Click Mark as Sent once the PO goes to the vendor.
  3. As deliveries arrive, use Receive Goods to log quantities received (status moves to PARTIAL or RECEIVED).
  4. Either click Convert to Bill (creates a normal AP Bill and GL entry — every line needs an expense account first), or Pay from Petty Cash for small purchases (creates an Expense Voucher and posts directly, bypassing AP).

Watch out for

  • A cancelled or already-billed PO cannot be converted or cancelled again; receiving cannot exceed the ordered quantity.
4.5 · Sidebar → Purchases → Expense Vouchers

Expense Vouchers

Records non-AP cash disbursements — petty cash, employee reimbursements, direct payments (utilities, rent), and cash-advance liquidations — for payees who may not even be a registered vendor.

Voucher TypePetty Cash, Reimbursement, Direct Payment, or Liquidation.
PayeeFree text — not limited to the vendor master, useful for gas stations, freelancers, or government agencies.
LinesDescription, optional COA Account, Amount, Receipt #.

How to do it

  1. Click New Voucher, choose a type, and enter payee, purpose, and line items.
  2. Click Submit to send it for approval.
  3. Approve or Reject (rejection requires a reason).
  4. Once approved, click Mark Paid and choose the funding account (Petty Cash 1011 by default) — this posts the GL entry.

Tips

  • Every voucher can be printed with signature blocks for Requested / Approved / Received By, and can carry receipt attachments.
4.6 · Sidebar → Purchases → Cash Requests

Cash Requests

Manages cash advances given to staff for anticipated purchases, from the initial request through to liquidating what was actually spent.

Requested For / Purpose / Needed ByHeader details, with an itemized estimate list.
ReleaseAmount to Release, Cash Source, Release Date — posts Dr 1104 Advances to Employees / Cr the chosen cash account.
LiquidateActual spend lines with receipts; computes variance — "Sukli to return" if less was spent than released, or "Reimburse" if more.

How to do it

  1. Click New Cash Request, list the estimated items, and Submit.
  2. Approve the request.
  3. Release the cash — choose the funding source; the advance posts to account 1104 and stays outstanding.
  4. After the employee spends it, click Liquidate and enter the actual receipts — Finara computes what is owed back or reimbursed.

Tips

  • The "Outstanding (1104)" summary card is the key control point — it shows every advance not yet liquidated.

Payroll

Employee records, running actual pay periods with statutory PH deductions, and a standalone calculator for quick estimates.

5.1 · Sidebar → Payroll → Employees

Employees

Maintains the employee roster — basic info, pay setup, and the government ID numbers required for BIR/SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG reporting — that payroll computation runs against.

Basic InfoEmployee No. (auto: EMP-001...), Department, Name, Position, Hire Date, Employment Type.
PayrollBasic Monthly Salary, Pay Frequency (Monthly / Semi-Monthly / Weekly) — includes a rough live deduction preview.
Govt. NumbersTIN, SSS No., PhilHealth No., Pag-IBIG No. — required for BIR Form 2316 and the statutory reports.

How to do it

  1. Click New Employee and complete the Basic Info, Payroll, and Govt. Numbers tabs.
  2. Save — the employee is now included in payroll computation while marked Active.
  3. Click any employee to open their profile drawer: quick stats, last 12 payslips, and a "BIR 2316" button to generate that employee's annual certificate for any of the last 5 years.

Watch out for

  • Inactive employees are excluded from payroll computation entirely.
5.2 · Sidebar → Payroll → Pay Periods

Pay Periods

Runs an actual payroll cycle through a fixed, government-audit-friendly lifecycle: Open → Compute → Approve → Mark Paid.

New PeriodPay Frequency (tag only), Start/End Date, Period Name (auto-suggested), Pay Date.
View PayslipsPer-employee Basic, Allowances, Gross, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, Tax, and Net Pay, with column totals.

How to do it

  1. Click New Period and set the dates.
  2. Click Compute — Finara runs statutory payroll for every active employee and fills in payslips (only allowed while status is OPEN).
  3. Review the payslips via View Payslips.
  4. Click Approve — this also auto-posts the GL accrual entry (Salaries & Wages, employer-share contributions, and payables).
  5. Click Mark Paid once disbursed — this status is final and cannot be reversed.

Tips

  • Print Payroll generates a full Payroll Register citing the exact rate basis used (2024 SSS table, PhilHealth 5%, Pag-IBIG 2%, TRAIN Law 2023 withholding schedule).

Watch out for

  • Compute, Approve, and Mark Paid can each only run from their specific prior status — trying to compute an already-computed period returns an error.
5.3 · Sidebar → Payroll → Calculator

Payroll Calculator

A standalone, real-time "what-if" estimator for take-home pay — nothing here is saved or affects actual employees or pay periods.

InputsBasic Monthly Salary, Non-Taxable Allowances, Overtime Pay, Pay Frequency.
OutputsNet pay breakdown, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG detail, Effective Tax Rate gauge, Annual Projection, 13th Month Pay, Employer Cost per Month, and a Monthly vs. Semi-Monthly vs. Weekly comparison table.

How to do it

  1. Enter a salary (or use a quick-pick button) and optional allowances/overtime.
  2. Choose a Pay Frequency.
  3. Read the results live — everything recomputes as you type.
  4. Click Reset to start over.

Watch out for

  • This page uses its own simplified copy of the SSS/tax tables and can differ slightly from the authoritative figures Compute Payroll produces on the Pay Periods page — treat it as an estimate, not a payslip.

Inventory & Fixed Assets

Tracking stocked items and physical goods movement, plus registering and depreciating long-term fixed assets.

6.1 · Sidebar → Inventory & Assets → Inventory → Stock on Hand

Stock on Hand

A read-only, filterable snapshot of current inventory quantity and value — the first stop before acting in Items or Transactions.

FiltersSearch (SKU/name), Category, Type (Product/Material/Supply/Asset).
Summary cardsTotal Items, Inventory Value, Low Stock count, Out of Stock count.

How to do it

  1. Open Stock on Hand and review the summary cards.
  2. Filter/search to find specific items.
  3. Rows highlight yellow (low stock, at or below reorder level) or red (out of stock) automatically.
  4. Click Print for a hard-copy report, or jump to Items/Transactions to act on what you see.
6.2 · Sidebar → Inventory & Assets → Inventory → Items

Items

The item catalog — SKU, pricing, and optional Chart-of-Accounts links (Inventory Asset, COGS, Revenue) used when transactions post to the GL.

SKUAuto-generated as SKU-0001... if left blank; locked once created.
Name (required), Category, Unit, Reorder Level, Cost/Selling PriceCore catalog fields.
GL linksInventory Asset Account, COGS Account, Revenue Account — all optional.

How to do it

  1. Optionally set up Categories first (via the Categories panel).
  2. Click New Item, enter a name (SKU auto-fills), unit, reorder level, and pricing.
  3. Optionally link the three GL accounts.
  4. Save — the item is ready to use on Transactions, Purchase Orders, and Quotations.

Watch out for

  • Duplicate SKUs are rejected. "Deactivate" is a soft delete — item history is preserved.
6.3 · Sidebar → Inventory & Assets → Inventory → Transactions

Transactions

Logs every physical stock movement — Stock In, Stock Out, Adjustment, Customer Return, Return to Supplier — building a running, auditable stock balance per item.

TypeStock In (IN), Stock Out (OUT), Adjustment, Customer Return (RETURN_IN), Return to Supplier (RETURN_OUT).
QuantityFor most types this is the amount moved; for Adjustment it is the actual counted stock — Finara computes the difference for you.

How to do it

  1. Click New Movement and pick a type.
  2. Choose the item and enter Quantity, Unit Cost, Date, and Reference.
  3. Submit — the item’s current stock updates immediately and a sequential transaction number is assigned.

Watch out for

  • Stock In / Return In post Dr Inventory / Cr Inventory Payable; Stock Out / Return Out post Dr COGS / Cr Inventory. Adjustments change quantity only — they never touch the GL, so book any related correction manually.
6.4 · Sidebar → Inventory & Assets → Inventory → Reports

Inventory Reports

Three printable analytical views for period-end review: Inventory Valuation, Low Stock Alert, and Movement Summary.

Inventory ValuationQty on hand × unit cost per item, subtotaled by category.
Low Stock AlertEvery item at or below its reorder level, sorted by largest shortage, with an estimated reorder cost.
Movement SummaryTotal In/Out, net adjustments, and cost In/Out per item over a date range.

How to do it

  1. Open Reports and pick a tab.
  2. Adjust the filter (Category, or a date range for Movement Summary).
  3. Review on screen, then click Print for a hard copy.
6.5 · Sidebar → Inventory & Assets → Fixed Assets

Fixed Assets

Registers long-term assets and runs monthly depreciation (Straight Line or Declining Balance), optionally auto-posting the depreciation journal entry.

New AssetName, Category, Acquisition Date, Cost, Salvage Value, Useful Life (months), Method.
Optional GL linksAsset Account, Depreciation Expense Account, Accumulated Depreciation Account — required for auto-posting.

How to do it

  1. Click New Asset and complete cost, useful life, method, and (optionally) the three GL accounts.
  2. Open the asset’s Schedule to see the full projected month-by-month depreciation.
  3. Click Record Next Period each period — this books the next month’s depreciation and, if GL accounts are set, posts the journal entry automatically.
  4. When retired or sold, click Dispose and enter the disposal amount — Finara computes the resulting gain or loss.

Watch out for

  • Once any depreciation has posted, only descriptive fields stay editable — cost, dates, method, and accounts lock. An asset with depreciation history cannot be deleted, only disposed.
07

Banking & Compliance

Reconciling the bank, filing the five core BIR forms, and tracking SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG/BIR remittances — the most compliance-dense part of Finara.

7.1 · Sidebar → Banking & Compliance → Bank Reconciliation

Bank Reconciliation

Matches the business’s internal bank transaction records against the bank’s monthly statement — the control that keeps every downstream report (including VAT and withholding filings) trustworthy.

Bank AccountAccount Name, Bank, Account Number, linked GL Account, Opening Balance.
Reconcile promptStatement Ending Balance and Statement Date.

How to do it

  1. Add a bank account and link it to a GL account.
  2. Log deposits and withdrawals as bank transactions.
  3. Click Reconcile and enter the statement balance/date.
  4. Tick every transaction that appears on the statement — Cleared and Difference totals update live.
  5. Click Complete. If it doesn't balance to the penny, Finara offers "Complete anyway?" as a forced override.

Watch out for

  • Once a reconciliation is completed, its transactions lock — they can no longer be deleted.
7.2 · Sidebar → Banking & Compliance → BIR Compliance → VAT Summary

VAT Summary (Form 2550M / 2550Q)

Computes Output VAT (on sales) against Input VAT (on purchases) to determine what’s payable — or creditable — for the Monthly (2550M) or Quarterly (2550Q) VAT return.

ModeMonthly (2550M) or Quarterly (2550Q).
PeriodYear + Month, or Year + Quarter.

How to do it

  1. Choose Monthly or Quarterly mode and select the period.
  2. Review the KPI cards (Total Sales, Output VAT, Input VAT, VAT Payable or Excess Input) and the Sales Composition breakdown.
  3. Check the 2550 Filing Summary box for the exact Part A / Part B figures to file.
  4. Drill into Supporting Transactions if a number looks off.

Tips

  • Due dates: the 20th of the following month for 2550M, the 25th of the month after quarter-end for 2550Q.
7.3 · Sidebar → Banking & Compliance → BIR Compliance → Withholding

Withholding Tax (Form 1601-C)

The Monthly Remittance Return of compensation tax withheld from employees, built directly from approved payroll.

PeriodYear + Month.
1601-C boxPart I — Compensation Details; Part II — Tax Computation and Total Amount Payable.

How to do it

  1. Select the month/year.
  2. Review the 1601-C form box and the per-payroll-period breakdown table.
  3. If the period is overdue, use Estimate Penalty (surcharge, interest, and compromise penalty) to gauge exposure.
  4. Remit via AAB or eBIRForms on or before the due date.

Watch out for

  • Due date is the 10th of the following month — overdue periods are flagged red with a penalty warning.
7.4 · Sidebar → Banking & Compliance → BIR Compliance → EWT Summary

EWT Summary (Form 1601-EQ)

The Quarterly Remittance Return of Expanded (Creditable) Withholding Tax deducted from payments to suppliers/vendors.

PeriodYear + Quarter.
ATC referenceAn expandable panel of official ATC codes and rates for context.

How to do it

  1. Select year and quarter.
  2. Review Total Payments, VATable Payments, and Total EWT to Remit.
  3. Expand a vendor row to see the supporting bills behind their EWT.
  4. Remit by the last day of the month following quarter-end.

Watch out for

  • Finara applies a flat 2% EWT (ATC WC200) to any bill whose net, VAT-exclusive amount exceeds ₱10,000 — other ATC rates shown in the reference table are informational only and are not separately computed.
7.5 · Sidebar → Banking & Compliance → BIR Compliance → Alphalist

Alphalist (Form 1604-C Annex)

The annual alphabetical listing of every employee’s compensation, deductions, and tax withheld, filed as an annex to the annual 1604-C return.

9.4 · Sidebar → Settings → Opening Balances

Opening Balances

A one-time, per-business setup screen that records a business’s day-one financial position as of its Books Start Date, so pre-cutover history doesn’t need re-entry.

PreconditionThe business must already have a Books Start Date set under Settings → Businesses.
LinesAccount, Debit, Credit — the difference auto-balances against account 3070 Opening Balance Equity.

How to do it

  1. Enter what customers still owe (AR), what’s still owed to vendors (AP), and actual cash/bank balances as of the cutover date.
  2. Confirm Total Debit equals Total Credit (the 3070 line balances it automatically).
  3. Click Post Opening Balances — this can only be done once per business.
  4. Review the reconciliation panel afterward, which compares AR/AP subledger totals against the posted opening figures.

9.5 · Sidebar → Settings → Database (ADMIN only)

Database — Backup, Restore & Reset

Exports and restores data by module, and provides two destructive reset paths — use with real caution, especially Full Database Reset.

Module-level ExportCheckbox-select modules → downloads a dated JSON backup file.
Restore from Backup FileUpload a .json file, pick which of its modules to restore, and choose whether to wipe existing data first.
Reset Selected ModulesType RESET to confirm; deletes data for chosen modules only (users/settings preserved).
Full Database ResetType RESET DATABASE plus your admin password; deletes essentially all transactional data.

How to do it

  1. Before any destructive action, run a Module-level Export (or Full Database Backup) and save the file somewhere safe.
  2. To restore, upload the backup, select modules, decide whether to wipe existing data first, and confirm.
  3. For a partial wipe, use Reset Selected Modules and type the exact confirmation phrase.

Watch out for

  • Restoring or resetting a module you don't fully back up first can leave dangling references (e.g. a bill pointing at a vendor that no longer exists) — always back up before restoring or resetting, and restore/reset a whole module together, not just part of it.
  • Full Database Reset is irreversible and deletes journal entries, bills, invoices, payroll, and the Chart of Accounts — users and settings are the only things preserved.
